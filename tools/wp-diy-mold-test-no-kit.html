DIY Mold Test With No Kit: Household Cotton Swab + Lab Report

You can start a mold test tonight without waiting for a box. Total Testing is a mail-in surface test: swab the stain with an unused household cotton swab, ship it with a prepaid FedEx label, and get an inspector-reviewed lab report that names species and growth level on that surface. What you need at home

  • Unused cotton swabs (one per sample location)
  • Clean zip bags
  • A marker to label room and date
  • A printer for the prepaid FedEx label and PDF chain of custody

After checkout you download those files from email or total-testing-diy.com. Step-by-step photos: how to collect a surface mold sample.

How the no-kit test works

  1. Choose Spot Check (2 samples), Extended (4), or Full House (6).
  2. Download the PDF chain of custody and prepaid FedEx label.
  3. Turn off nearby fans. Roll a clean swab across the visible stain. Seal and label the bag. Do not scrape the growth.
  4. Pack the bags with the completed form. Drop at FedEx.
  5. View the inspector-reviewed lab report in the portal 24–48 business hours after the lab receives the samples.

What the lab report is — and is not

The report identifies mold on the surfaces you swabbed. The EPA notes there are no federal mold limits, so a swab cannot prove a house “passes” a government standard. The CDC does not recommend home testing as a way to predict who will get sick.

Visible growth is still a reason to swab when you need a written record for a landlord or buyer, or a species ID before you hire remediation. Read do I need to test if I can already see it? and how to test for mold in your house.
